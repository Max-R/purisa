# CIB research keeps Purisa's graph, rejects its thresholds

*Research report, 2026-09-22. Synthesised from five research notes in `research_notes/CIB detection state of the art 2026/`, read against `purisa-jev-plan/VISION.md`, `validation/RESULTS.md` and `docs/spikes/2026-09-22-jev.md`. Vendor and self-reported claims are flagged inline. Almost every quantitative result in this literature is measured on X/Twitter; nothing has been evaluated on Bluesky or Hacker News, so every transfer claim below is inference.*

The 2024–2026 literature has **not** diverged from Purisa's skeleton — a per-window co-action graph over synchronised posting, shared URLs, text similarity, hashtags and co-replies, followed by community detection, is still exactly what the field's own survey, the USC-ISI pipelines and the surviving open toolkits do ([Cinus et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257v2); [Luceri et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09884); [CooRTweet](https://github.com/nicolarighetti/CooRTweet)). It has diverged sharply from Purisa's *parameterisation and framing*: fixed absolute thresholds (90 s, TF-IDF > 0.8, density 0.3) are the practice the survey calls "typically selected arbitrarily", hourly bins are not how anyone computes synchrony, single-layer Louvain has been overtaken by Leiden and multiplex methods, a weighted additive 0–100 score has no precedent, and — most consequentially — the field now treats coordination, authenticity and harm as orthogonal, so an "organic ↔ engineered" axis read off structure alone is not defensible as an *authenticity* claim ([Mannocci et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257); [Rogers & Righetti 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/29768624251369784)). The saturation Phase 0 found is not a Purisa bug but the known behaviour of unnormalised co-occurrence, and the literature's remedies (popularity normalisation, shorter windows, null models, topic-and-time-matched controls) are cheap and specific. The datasets to run them on exist: OSoMe's 26-campaign Zenodo release ships **matched organic controls**, which converts "IRA recall" into precision and false-positive rate in one step ([Seckin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10609)). Bluesky + Hacker News remains the right access choice — Bluesky is the only major platform with a free complete firehose and it now hosts documented Russian operations — but HN has no ground truth at all and should be demoted to a secondary target. The Jev decision is consistent with the evidence on *how* to classify (small typed models, no generation channel to inject into) but the evidence on *whether* semantic labels improve organic-vs-coordinated separation is absent; semantics adds coverage, not accuracy, and the roadmap should be re-ordered so the structural fixes and the negative-control leg land before Phase 2.

## What has not changed: the five signals, the graph, and validation-first

Purisa's signal set maps one-to-one onto the canonical repertoire. The 2024 survey counts co-sharing as the most common co-action (31 works), then co-URL (~28), co-hashtag (~20) and co-reply (6), with text-match, co-mention and media signals as extensions ([Cinus et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257v2)). The WWW 2024 and 2025 USC-ISI pipelines use Co-Retweet, Co-URL, Hashtag Sequence, Fast Retweet and Text Similarity ([Luceri et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09884); [Pantè et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17344)); the QUT Coordination Network Toolkit ships co_retweet, co_tweet, co_similar_tweet, co_link, co_reply and co_post ([CNT README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QUT-Digital-Observatory/coordination-network-toolkit/main/README.md)). Nothing Purisa uses has been dropped. Purisa's reply-to-same-parent edge, which looked like the odd one out in 2021, is now well motivated: Mannocci et al. find co-reply "captures entirely distinct structures (zero common communities with RTW in UK)" and that "none of them can be excluded a priori" ([Mannocci et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12108)), and Pote et al. make coordinated replies a first-class detection target on IO data ([Pote et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19272)).

The "co-action graph → community detection" architecture is still the state of practice, not a legacy design. The survey does not review GNN-based coordination detection at all; the 2025 TikTok, Telegram and cross-platform studies all use bipartite-projection cosine-similarity networks with pruning ([Cinus et al. WWW 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22716); [Luceri et al. TikTok 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10867v2)). Louvain remains the single most-used algorithm (~22 works in the survey). Per-window graph construction is standard; a September 2026 sweep across 14 IO campaigns concludes "Adjacent non-overlapping windows appear sufficient" and that stride and overlap barely matter ([Panayiotou et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21959)). Purisa is architecturally in-family with CooRTweet and CNT — and those are batch libraries with no ingestion, scheduler or dashboard, which is exactly where Purisa differs.

The LLM era has not invalidated behaviour-first detection; if anything it has strengthened it. Every documented LLM-powered operation of 2025–2026 still ran an old structural playbook: OpenAI's "Sneer Review" was "a post — followed by a series of reply comments from other accounts", "High Five" posted thousands of ≤10-word comments from accounts with 0–10 followers, and Meta's Iranian network had accounts "comment on the network's own content" ([OpenAI June 2025 PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/threat-intelligence-reports/5f73af09-a3a3-4a55-992e-069237681620/disrupting-malicious-uses-of-ai-june-2025.pdf); [Meta Q1 2025 ATR](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q1-2025-Adversarial-threat-report/)). Meta's H2 2026 position (self-reported) is that "behavioral detection, coordination analysis, and network-level enforcement continue to identify and disrupt threats — regardless of how sophisticated the AI-generated content layer has become" ([Meta H2 2026 ATR](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/H2-2026-adversarial-threat-report)), and Anthropic's September 2026 report (self-reported) describes 250+ commenting accounts and 1,000+ fake X accounts with "warm-up logic" — text got fluent, structure did not change ([Anthropic threat report, Sept 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026)). Attackers are visibly adapting to *text* detectors (em-dash removal, uncensored self-hosted Llama for CopyCop) but there is no primary source showing adaptation to behavioural detectors ([Recorded Future on CopyCop](https://www.recordedfuture.com/research/copycop-deepens-its-playbook-with-new-websites-and-targets)).

Finally, VISION §7's validation-first posture is precisely the de facto publication norm. There is no shared coordination leaderboard; the survey admits methods are "assessed through post-hoc analyses rather than standardized quantitative benchmarks" ([Mannocci et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257v2)), and the current bar — as practised by Cinus et al. — is precision/recall against an external labelled IO set plus manual review tables in an appendix ([Cinus et al. WWW 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22716v4)). Purisa's published thresholds, seeded clustering and Phase 0 harness are already aligned with that bar. Keep all of this.

## What has diverged: parameters, algorithm, score, and the meaning of "coordinated"

**Fixed thresholds are the criticised practice.** The survey names three edge-filtering families — fixed thresholds, statistical validation, and timing of co-actions — and says "similarity and centrality thresholds are typically selected arbitrarily, without a strong underlying theoretical motivation" ([Mannocci et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257)). Purisa's `>0.8`, `0.3` density and `90 s` are all in the weakest family. What replaced them: per-trace percentile thresholds found by grid search (Luceri's optimised cut-offs ranged from the 50th to 96th percentile, versus the 99.5th used by prior work), eigenvector-centrality node pruning that lifted precision by +0.42 on average ([Luceri et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09884)); per-platform percentile grids (Facebook 50th, X 85th, Telegram 99th) selected by minimum component density ([Cinus et al. WWW 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22716)); a permutation null that shuffles which object each post points to, 1,000 times per indicator, with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction ([null-model-coord](https://github.com/manitapote/null-model-coord)); and a mixture-of-exponentials fit to inter-share times that put the Facebook cut-off at **27 seconds on average**, with the authors arguing that thresholds imported from other contexts were assumed "without evidence" to transfer ([Yang et al., Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-00233-w)).

**Hourly bins are not how synchrony is computed.** CooRTweet's `detect_groups` filters pairwise time differences directly against `time_window` (default 10 s; vignettes use 30–60 s) ([CooRTweet docs](https://rdrr.io/cran/CooRTweet/man/detect_groups.html)); CNT's default is 60 s for all network types; Schoch et al.'s preferred threshold is one minute, with a ceiling "below 8 hours" before false positives climb ([Schoch et al. 2022](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/86005/ssoar-scientrep-2022-schoch_et_al-Coordination_patterns_reveal_online_political.pdf?sequence=1)); Iannucci et al. drop windows entirely for an exponential decay kernel e^(−βΔt) normalised by (n_k − 1) co-actors ([Iannucci et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19677)). Purisa's 90 s is at the loose end of the range and, applied inside an hourly bin, misses cross-boundary pairs. Panayiotou et al. find precision is highest at 10 s–1 min and "generally decreases as the window length grows" ([Panayiotou et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21959)).

**TF-IDF cosine is a lexical near-duplicate test the field has abandoned.** Text similarity was the *weakest* standalone indicator in the WWW 2024 evaluation (AUC 0.52 ± 0.05, as low as 0.47) against Co-URL's 0.72 ([Luceri et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09884)); the 2025 pipelines use multilingual sentence transformers at thresholds from 0.7 (TikTok captions) to 0.95 (per-user-pair averages within one-day windows) ([Luceri et al. TikTok 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10867v1); [Cinus et al. WWW 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22716)). Righetti's own summary of the CooRnet successor work: "duplicate messages becoming less effective; coordinated actors can produce numerous variations of the same message" ([Righetti / VERA.AI](https://www.nicolarighetti.net/conferences/defending-information-integrity-in-the-age-of-generative-al-the-problem-of-coordinated-manipulation-insights-from-vera-ai/)). In simulation, LLM IO agents kept content similarity at 0.89–0.91 versus 0.62 for organic agents even though no two posts were copies ([Orlando et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25003)) — narrative convergence survives paraphrase; lexical overlap does not. Note the tension with RESULTS.md: text similarity flags ~6% of IRA posts and "is doing the real discriminating work" there, but on a troll-only corpus nothing can discriminate; on labelled IO-vs-control data this is the signal that discriminates least.

**Louvain has a better-connected, faster successor, and flattening loses structure.** On a 3.2M-node Twitter mention network Leiden gave mean modularity 0.7453 vs Louvain 0.5942 and ran in 22.5 s vs 80.5 s; the authors restate that "the Louvain algorithm may generate arbitrarily badly connected communities" ([Sliwa et al. 2024](https://nm.wu.ac.at/nm/strembeck/publications/tempweb24.pdf)). That matters specifically for Purisa because a `min_density 0.3` guard applied after Louvain can reject communities for being internally disconnected rather than for being sparse. Mannocci et al. compare monomodal, flattened and multiplex fusion and say "Avoid flattening": union fusion "risks substantial loss of structural information" while intersection collapsed 1.18M users to 257 nodes ([Mannocci et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12108)). Purisa's single fused NetworkX graph is a union flatten.

**The additive 0–100 score has no precedent; spike detection on it has no literature.** The survey states "the majority of existing works leverage a single co-action" and offers "No Composite Scoring Framework" ([Cinus et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257v2)). The nearest defensible scalars are the null model's `max_n_signal` (count of indicators significant at FDR-corrected p < α, an ordinal 0–5) or multiplex membership with per-layer significance ([null-model-coord](https://github.com/manitapote/null-model-coord)). No 2024–2026 paper studies anomaly detection over a coordination-score series; the closest practice compares per-window counts against a reference period ([Journal of Big Data 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9913025/)), and Panayiotou et al. note the number of communities "increases with window length in almost every campaign" — a count whose distribution depends on the window, which is why MAD should run on counts, not on a threshold-dependent composite.

**The largest divergence is semantic: coordination is not inauthenticity.** The survey defines coordination, authenticity and harmfulness as independent dimensions, with "activists, social movements, and mutual support groups" as authentic coordination ([Mannocci et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257)). Rogers & Righetti's Facebook detection surfaced "predominantly" media groups, activists and ad networks and they conclude that "by coordination tactics alone, [they] have not been able to distinguish between what could be called coordinated authentic and inauthentic behaviour without considering the actors or sources" ([Rogers & Righetti 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/29768624251369784)). Iannucci et al. state their method "requires human analyst intervention to distinguish malicious from grassroots coordination" ([Iannucci et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19677)); Panayiotou et al. that grassroots mobilisation and orchestration "can produce similar network signatures"; Pote et al. warn coordinated replies may be "regular social media users organizing among themselves for activism" ([Pote et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19272)). K-pop fan clubs run timed like/reply campaigns that platforms already downgrade as "bot activity" ([Xiao et al., CHI 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10670)). Meta's enforcement definition of CIB is actor-based ("false identities"), not timing-based ([Meta Threat Reporting](https://transparency.meta.com/metasecurity/threat-reporting/)). VISION §5's moat — "high volume, high coordination → brigade" — is a coordination measurement being sold as an authenticity measurement. The literature says the axis must be labelled *structural coordination*, with actor type and covertness assessed separately.

**Signals Purisa lacks that the field has added.** Hashtag *sequence* rather than single-hashtag overlap, co-domain as a coarser URL signal, co-mention ([Cinus et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01257v2)); and, decisively for Bluesky, account-level signals — creation-time bursts, co-follow clustering, handle-generation patterns, PDS/DID provenance — which are what the documented Bluesky networks were actually caught with ([clemensv/bskyhygiene](https://github.com/clemensv/bskyhygiene); [Bluesky 2025 Transparency Report](https://bsky.social/about/blog/01-29-2026-transparency-report-2025), self-reported). The consistent LLM-era structural tell is *engagement asymmetry*: many posts, self-replies, near-zero organic uptake ([Graphika "Cheap Tricks"](https://www.graphika.com/reports/cheap-tricks), vendor).

## Saturation is expected, and the fixes are normalisation, controls and shorter windows

RESULTS.md reports median `sync_rate = 1.0` in every window, even at 53 posts/hour where the median inter-post gap (~68 s) is already inside the 90 s window. The literature explains this directly and has measured it. Weber & Falzon showed star-shaped "highly coordinating communities" grew consistently as the window widened and warned that "the longer the time window chosen, the more co-activities are captured, but the more filtering is required to remove accidental coordination" ([Weber & Falzon 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.02588)). Panayiotou et al. show longer windows "increasingly merg[e] labelled inauthentic actors with organic users who repost the same content over longer time spans" ([Panayiotou et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21959)). Purisa treats every co-occurrence as equal evidence; the field's central correction is that co-acting on something 10,000 accounts also touched is nearly worthless. Iannucci et al. normalise by popularity so that "actions performed by many users contribute less than actions shared by fewer users" ([Iannucci et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19677)); OSoMe's indicators are all "IDF-weighted cosine similarity" ([null-model-coord](https://github.com/manitapote/null-model-coord)); Luceri et al. TF-IDF-weight the user–entity bipartite graph before projection ([Luceri et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09884)). Wohlert et al. found "temporal proximity of interactions has not been as informative as the frequency of such interactions" ([Wohlert et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05868)).

The false-positive archetypes are known and all four will appear on Bluesky. Event reactions: when Vargas et al. tested on unseen legitimate communities, precision fell from **0.985 to 0.641** (worst 0.283), and three of the ten most-misclassified days were the US Congress reacting to the State of the Union, the national-emergency declaration, and UK MPs on Remembrance Sunday — "false positives in coordinated activity detection also happen when the online world reacts to real-world events" ([Vargas et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.13466)). Hub accounts: Loru et al. removed 12 of 5,450 top-scoring accounts (8 politicians, 4 news outlets) as false positives because *other users synchronised with them* ([Loru et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01283)). Media networks and ad rings: the Rogers & Righetti majority. Generic duplicates: embedding-based methods flagged ~39% of "coordinated" duplicate campaigns as generic text; removing them shrank the largest component from 428 to 166 nodes and raised density from 0.098 to 0.173 ([arXiv 2609.13671](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13671)). Retweet-derived edges produce "the highest number of false positives across the three measures" ([Schoch et al. 2022](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/86005/ssoar-scientrep-2022-schoch_et_al-Coordination_patterns_reveal_online_political.pdf?sequence=1)).

The evidence that the problem is solvable is equally specific. With topic-matched organic samples in the targeted country, Schoch et al. detect ">80 per cent of the astroturfing accounts, while the number of false positives hovers around 1 per cent" at a one-minute threshold, and the organic German #allesindenArm campaign was "roughly equivalent to the random samples and therefore clearly distinguishable" from astroturfing ([Schoch et al. 2022](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/86005/ssoar-scientrep-2022-schoch_et_al-Coordination_patterns_reveal_online_political.pdf?sequence=1)). Pantè et al. re-tested published claims of inter-state coordination against matched controls with Mann–Whitney and Bonferroni correction over 197 tests and found *none* were significant (Qatar–Egypt/UAE p = 0.49; Armenia–Russia p = 0.39) — "coordination" without a baseline can be entirely artefactual ([Pantè et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17344)). The realistic ceiling for an unsupervised pipeline on labelled IO-vs-control data is precision above 0.9 at recall around 0.1 ([Cinus et al. WWW 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22716)), or AUC ~0.8 for the fused network / null model ([Luceri et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09884); [null-model-coord](https://github.com/manitapote/null-model-coord)). Purisa's 97–100% recall at K≥3 is therefore not the headline — it is the cheap half; every method reaches high recall on a corpus that is coordinated by construction. The number that will make or break the tool is the FPR on a matched organic slice, and no published work reports one for fandoms, sports or breaking news — a project that does would be ahead of the literature.

## Datasets and validation routes that actually exist in 2026

The X/Twitter IO archive that the older literature relied on is gone: the TMRC covered 115,474 accounts and >100M tweets across 57 operations, but Bastos writes of access in the past tense and OSoMe's reproducibility checklist states "The IO data is no longer available to the public" ([Bastos 2025](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/34622/8/bastos-2025-visual-identities-in-troll-farms-the-twitter-moderation-research-consortium.pdf); [Seckin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10609)); the researcher's direct fetch of X's page returned HTTP 402. VISION §7's access caveat is confirmed — do not plan on it.

What survives is better for Purisa's purpose than the raw archive. **OSoMe's Zenodo release** (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14141549) covers 26 campaigns from 16 state actors with an `is_control` column, hashed PII, and control accounts sampled by matching IO hashtags on matching dates — e.g. Russia 1: 3,293 IO / 31,303 control accounts; China 1: 699 / 42,120; Cuba 503 / 30,099; Spain 216 / 1,681 ([Seckin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10609)). This is the benchmark Cinus, Iannucci and Panayiotou all validate on. Its caveats are stated by the authors: controls are biased toward generic hashtags and hyperactive accounts, timelines are cropped at 100 posts (temporal misalignment), and controls "may have non-IO coordinated activity" (#crypto spammers) that a correct detector would be penalised for finding. The Zenodo licence could not be verified (API 403); check before redistributing anything beyond aggregate metrics. Loading this through the existing `validation/load_ira.py` path with `source_query = "validation:osome:<campaign>"` turns Phase 0's recall table into a precision/recall/FPR table with a built-in negative class.

The **South Korean NIS 2012** campaign is the only ground truth not produced by a platform's own unauditable detector — the account list came from court proceedings ([Schoch et al. 2022](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/86005/ssoar-scientrep-2022-schoch_et_al-Coordination_patterns_reveal_online_political.pdf?sequence=1)) — and CooRTweet publishes numbers on it: "Using a 60-second optimal time interval, CooRTweet could detect 731 (91%) and 139 (17%) of the NIS accounts in separate co-retweet and co-tweet networks" ([Righetti et al., CCR 2025](https://www.aup-online.com/content/journals/10.5117/CCR2025.1.7.RIGH)). That is a directly comparable external calibration target for Purisa's sync and text channels. Whether the list is currently redistributable was not verified.

For known-organic coordinated communities — the stress test the axis needs — the precedents are institutional and event-driven: Vargas et al. used UK Parliament (568 accounts), US Congress (527), academics (818) and random users (543) as legitimate baselines, oversampled to 1:9 and 1:3 to mimic base rates, and reported 95% CIs over three seeds ([Vargas et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.13466)); Schoch et al. used #allesindenArm. On Bluesky these must be built from the firehose (a breaking-news hour, a sports final, a protest hashtag), and the literature has no FPR numbers for any of them. For a hard-timestamp coordination ground truth, PumpSense provides 283,017 Telegram messages with 2,246 labelled pump-start announcements ([arXiv 2605.09431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09431)) — a rare "known coordination time" structure for testing a sync window; licence unverified.

Bluesky itself has **no labelled CIB dataset**. The usable weak labels are the documented operations: Alliance4Europe identified "more than 100 accounts" of Doppelgänger "systematically replying to regular users' posts" on 17 January 2025, with 4,900+ posts in a week ([Alliance4Europe](https://alliance4europe.eu/doppelganger-bluesky)); Clemson documented Matryoshka/Storm-1679 impersonating German figures during the February 2025 election ([Clemson MFH](https://open.clemson.edu/mfh_ci_reports/12/)); the `bskyhygiene` repository publishes daily blocklists for swarms of 195 to 74,038 accounts derived from creation-time clustering, co-follow and handle patterns ([clemensv/bskyhygiene](https://github.com/clemensv/bskyhygiene), individual investigator, unaudited). Bluesky's own figure of 3,619 suspected-IO accounts removed in 2025 is self-reported and the accounts are not listed ([Bluesky 2025 Transparency Report](https://bsky.social/about/blog/01-29-2026-transparency-report-2025)). PolitiSky24 offers stance labels and interaction graphs, not coordination labels ([arXiv 2506.07606](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07606)). Hacker News has nothing: no academic study of vote rings or comment brigading was found, the ring detector is deliberately opaque ("I'd never claim that our software catches everything" — [dang, HN item 22761897](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22761897)), and the Firebase API exposes aggregate scores, not votes.

For synthetic injection, the cleanest template is Guo, Hu & Zhang (September 2026): inject synthetic identities with known coalition membership into real Amazon review streams, use "exact clean twins" as counterfactual controls, sweep the attack and report ROC-AUC (0.500 → 0.797 as account reuse increases), with code released ([arXiv 2609.13407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13407)). It transfers directly: replace reviews with posts and coalitions with N accounts posting within W seconds into a real Bluesky hour. LLM-agent simulators can generate campaigns but no paper yet uses them as a detector benchmark ([arXiv 2605.28725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28725)); treat them as generators for injection, not as ground truth. For adjudication, the best-documented protocol labelled 1,000 tweets with an LLM first pass and had "two independent human annotators re-label 200 tweets, achieving 97.0% raw agreement and a Cohen's κ of 0.919" — for a content property, not the harder "is this inauthentic?" judgement, where no κ exists ([arXiv 2609.13671](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13671)). Vargas's date-level error review (inspect the ten most-misclassified days against the news) is the other precedent.

Two constraints on publication. Pseudonymised data "remains personal data under GDPR as long as a re-identification key or equivalent additional information exists anywhere" ([Manchester guidance 2026](https://documents.manchester.ac.uk/display.aspx?DocID=24560)), and the November 2024 Hugging Face episode — 1M Bluesky posts with DIDs published from the firehose and withdrawn after backlash — shows Bluesky's community norms are stricter than its technical openness ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/bluesky-posts-machine-learning-ai-datasets-hugging-face/)). Practitioner publication standards converge on stating confidence as high/moderate/low with reasons, never attributing from co-sharing alone, and publishing replicable evidence ([Nimmo, Verification Handbook](https://datajournalism.com/read/handbook/verification-3/investigating-platforms/11-network-analysis-and-attribution); [DFRLab FIAT](https://dfrlab.org/2024/10/23/dfrlab-launches-fiat-2024/)). The litigated risk to researchers has been platform retaliation (X v. CCDH dismissed on speech grounds; X v. Media Matters survived dismissal on a "manipulated images" methodology theory) rather than defamation by a labelled account ([Knight Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/cases/x-corp-v-center-for-countering-digital-hate); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/08/29/judge-rules-against-media-matters-request-to-dismiss-lawsuit-by-x.html)) — the defence is exactly FIAT's transparency criterion: publish parameters, code and evidence so a finding is replicable analysis, not asserted fact.

## Bluesky holds as the primary platform; Hacker News is unvalidatable

Bluesky is the only major platform where a single unaffiliated developer can ingest 100% of public activity, free, in real time: the firehose needs no authentication, Jetstream is operated by Bluesky PBC and "free to use" at ~25 GB/month for zstd posts-only ([bsky.network firehose docs](https://bsky.network/docs/consuming-the-firehose); [jazco.dev on Jetstream](https://jazco.dev/2024/09/24/jetstream/)). Every competitor route is gated: X removed its academic tier in 2023, moved to pay-per-use in February 2026 at $0.005 per read capped at 2M reads/month (third-party trackers), was fined €120M in December 2025 with €40M for obstructing researcher access, and its DSA remediation accepted in July 2026 still leaves "appropriate volumes" undefined ([TechPolicy.Press, Feb 2026](https://www.techpolicy.press/what-the-x-fine-reveals-about-data-access-under-article-40-of-the-digital-services-act/); [TechPolicy.Press, July 2026](https://www.techpolicy.press/what-xs-corrected-action-under-the-dsa-fixes-on-data-access-and-whats-left-open/)). Reddit requires accredited-university affiliation and IRB approval; Meta Content Library excludes for-profit users and needs CASD review; TikTok's Research API needs non-profit academic affiliation ([Reddit for Researchers](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/49381918834964-Reddit-for-Researchers-Program); [Meta Content Library access](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/content-library-and-api/get-access); [TikTok Research API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api)). The DSA Article 40 route requires research-institution or not-for-profit affiliation and a funding disclosure ([EC FAQ](https://algorithmic-transparency.ec.europa.eu/news/faqs-dsa-data-access-researchers-2025-07-03_en)); a fiscal sponsor or academic partner would unlock several doors at once, but that is a strategic choice, not a Phase 0 blocker.

Bluesky is also now a real target, which was not clear when the platform choice was made. It grew to 41.41M users and 1.41B posts in 2025 (self-reported), removed 3,619 suspected-IO accounts mostly "linked to foreign state-aligned actors, including actors likely operating from Russia", and described detection that "relies primarily on behavioral patterns and technical indicators—such as coordination signals and account relationships—rather than on content analysis alone" ([Bluesky 2025 Transparency Report](https://bsky.social/about/blog/01-29-2026-transparency-report-2025)). Doppelgänger and Matryoshka were independently documented there by civil-society analysts working with manual OSINT and DISARM — and Alliance4Europe explicitly asked Bluesky to "Develop automated systems detecting coordinated inauthentic behavior based on technical infrastructure" ([Alliance4Europe](https://alliance4europe.eu/doppelganger-bluesky)). No free tool monitors Bluesky continuously for journalists: Junkipedia covers 14 platforms but not Bluesky or HN ([ATI Junkipedia](https://ati.io/junkipedia/)); OSoMe's Coordiscope and OSoMeNet are exploratory visualisers, not alerting systems ([OSoMe blog](https://osome.iu.edu/research/blog/exploring-the-latest-osome-tools)); the labeler ecosystem is ~707 live labelers of which 25 define a spam label and 8 a bot label (vendor census) ([Agent Sky](https://useagentsky.com/blog/what-is-a-bluesky-labeler)). The demand is stated and the space is empty.

Two Bluesky-specific caveats. First, the documented networks were caught with account-graph signals Purisa does not compute — bulk creation on a single day, default profiles, co-follow rings, multi-PDS self-hosting (the "Haruhwa" network spanned 8 PDSs and ≥74,038 accounts per bskyhygiene) — so a Bluesky-first roadmap needs creation-time, follow-graph and PDS/DID provenance features. Second, Bluesky-bridged Fediverse content is an emerging attribution problem the platform itself flags. Both argue for Mastodon as the cheapest next source (open API, no vetting, captures Threads users via bridging), with the instance-policy caveats documented in the Fediverse ethics literature ([arXiv 2505.07606](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07606v1)).

Hacker News is a different case. Its API is free, unlimited and MIT-licensed ([HackerNews/API](https://github.com/HackerNews/API)), and the comment-brigading niche is plausibly under-served (HN's defences target *vote* rings; the March 2026 changes — an AI-comment ban and Show HN restrictions — are content rules, not network detection: [HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html); [HN item 47340079](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47340079)). But there is no ground truth, no academic literature, no vote data, and moderation is deliberately opaque to avoid gaming. HN cannot host any of the validation legs. Keep it as a low-cost secondary source with an explicit "unvalidated" label in any output; do not let it consume Phase 0–1 effort.

## The Jev layer fits the evidence on *how*, not on *whether*

The evidence supports the shape of the decision. Cost-aware model selection finds fine-tuned encoders at $12–33 per million requests versus $843–1,175 for frontier zero-shot, with the conclusion that "fine-tuned encoders emerge as robust and efficient decision engines… while LLMs are more effectively positioned as complementary components" ([arXiv 2602.06370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06370)); Google's trust-and-safety survey recommends "small, fast models to scan most content, reserving" LLMs for hard cases and labelling ([arXiv 2604.00323](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00323)). The security argument is the strongest one: on TwiBot-20, prompt injections embedded in the tweets themselves cut open 7–9B LLM detectors' accuracy by up to 48%, and standard defences "degrade performance on clean data" ([Orenstein & Birman 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15893)); known-answer injection detectors can be driven to 0% ([arXiv 2507.05630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05630)). A typed-decision model with no generation channel narrows that surface — but does not close it, and the spike is right to say so: TypeSafe's own jaggedness notes state that "state is not treated as hostile", so the adversarial slice in Phase 2a is load-bearing, not optional. The spike's other honesty should be preserved in VISION: Jev's 67.8% accuracy is on the vendor's own four-workflow suite, scored against a consensus of frontier LLMs, with **no independent reproduction** — a vendor claim, not a benchmark.

The evidence does not support the premise that semantic labels will improve organic-vs-coordinated *separation*. No 2024–2026 paper reports precision/recall for coordinated clusters with and without semantic edges on the same labelled data. The nearest ablation is for bots: account-history features alone give ROC-AUC 0.977, content-only 0.830, fusion 0.981 — a +0.0035 gain the author calls small — and under full rewriting of bot text the content F1 falls 0.733 → 0.644 while behavioural F1 holds at 0.916 ([Katyal 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26127)). What semantics demonstrably does is find *different* actors: the overlap between co-URL and text-similarity detections was 1.3% / 0.5% / 0% on Telegram / X / Facebook ([Cinus et al. WWW 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22716)). Semantics adds coverage of campaign types (content laundering, pile-ons) that URL and timing edges miss; it does not sharpen the axis. And organic collectives converge on narratives too, so the survivors as discriminators are engagement asymmetry, reply-ring structure, account history and cross-window persistence — semantic edges should raise a cluster's evidence only in combination with those.

Three consequences for the Phase 2–3 design. First, the literature's pattern is to use semantic identity as an *edge definition* ("same complex narrative within a window" is treated as "a strong signal of inauthentic coordination" — [arXiv 2604.00323](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00323)), not as a per-node feature; VISION's Phase 3 target-directed burst emitting `SimilarityResult`s already follows this, and the (target, stance) pair within a window is the right edge. Second, the per-post label is not evidence; the cluster-level aggregate is. Zero-shot LLM stance reaches only "fair" human agreement (Fleiss κ 0.65, Krippendorff α 0.61 after removing uncertain labels — [PLOS ONE 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0307741)), collapses to 42.3% accuracy in the implicit-stance complexity band for GPT-4o-mini ([SICI, arXiv 2606.13189](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13189)), swings 20–40 points across semantically equivalent prompts unless majority-voted over ~10 phrasings ([Liu 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.16413)), and both humans and LLMs fail on sarcasm ([arXiv 2501.02532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02532)) — which is where brigading content lives. The `pile_on` preset's `polarity: negative` filter will be weakest exactly on ironic pile-ons; the Phase 2a gate should add a sarcasm/implicit-stance slice, a per-label calibration curve, and an abstain band, since the only calibrated classifiers in the sources are tree-based behavioural ones. Third, Jev does not produce embeddings, and narrative clustering as practised (DiNaM: LLM filter → intent-focused embedding → UMAP → HDBSCAN → LLM cluster label, F1 0.77 / precision 0.66 at the post level — [arXiv 2605.14354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14354)) needs a sentence-transformer regardless. That same embedding model is what should replace TF-IDF in the structural engine, so it belongs in Phase 1, not Phase 3, and Jev's role narrows to classification over code-proposed candidates, which is what the spike already calls "retrieve then judge". The deferred cluster-triage judge should stay deferred: the closest measured result for an LLM asked "is this cluster coordinated?" is F1 0.57 on serialised propagation trees ([arXiv 2501.11849](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11849)), and VISION's rule that its output never enters the structural score is correct.

## Conclusion

The honest reading of the 2024–2026 literature is that Purisa's Phase 0 result was measuring the wrong half of the problem, and the field already knows it: recall on a corpus that is coordinated by construction is the cheap number every method achieves, while the number that establishes a tool — the false-positive rate against topic-and-time-matched organic behaviour — is one that almost nobody publishes, and the dataset to compute it now ships with the negative class built in. The structural engine does not need to be replaced; it needs its co-occurrences normalised by popularity, its thresholds turned into percentiles or p-values, its text channel moved from lexical to semantic, and its output relabelled from "engineered" to "structurally coordinated" with the actor question handed to a human. Those four changes are each a few hundred lines, and every one is cited above.

The deeper implication for the roadmap is an ordering change rather than a scope change. The semantic layer was justified in VISION as the thing that lets Purisa handle pile-ons and disinformation; the evidence says it will extend *what kinds* of campaigns the graph can see, not *whether* a given cluster is organic. So the structural fixes and the negative-control leg should land before Jev is wired in, the sentence-embedding store should move from Phase 3 to Phase 1 because the engine needs it first, and the Bluesky-native account-graph signals — the ones that actually caught Doppelgänger and Matryoshka — deserve a detector ahead of narrative clustering. Done in that order, the project ends up with something the literature does not currently contain: a published FPR on organic Bluesky bursts, a continuous detector on the one major platform whose data is free and whose watchdogs have asked for exactly this.
